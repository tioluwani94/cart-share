# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

The primary user is the person carrying most of a household's grocery-planning mental load. They remember what is running low, coordinate with another household member, decide what belongs in the next shop, and often manage the budget. They usually open the app briefly in a kitchen, while commuting, or between other tasks. The initial launch focuses on UK households, while the product model remains ready for additional markets.

The secondary user is the person carrying out the shop. They may be in a supermarket aisle with one hand available, poor connectivity, and little patience for setup or interpretation. For the initial beta, the existing two-member household model remains in scope; larger household membership is a later expansion.

## Product Purpose

Our Pantry reduces the cognitive load of grocery shopping. It remembers a household's recurring products, notices when they may need attention, asks a small and timely question, and prepares the next shop for online handoff or fast physical shopping. The first market is the UK; international expansion follows evidence of retention rather than launching multiple incomplete localisations at once.

Success means households no longer rely on one person to remember every staple, accept a useful majority of restock suggestions, complete shopping with fewer forgotten items, and return for the next planning cycle without maintaining an exact pantry inventory.

## Positioning

Our Pantry remembers your household's grocery rhythm and prepares the next shop before you have to think about it.

## Brand Personality

Calm, thoughtful, and warm. The product should feel quietly attentive rather than clever or performative. Its voice is direct, reassuring, and specific: it explains why something is being suggested, uses ordinary household language, and never presents an estimate as certainty.

The interaction character takes Apple Reminders' familiarity, Things' calm hierarchy, and Monzo's approachable financial clarity. Delight is reserved for meaningful completion moments.

## Anti-references

- Cartoonish or game-like pantry management with constant celebration.
- Dashboard-heavy productivity software that makes grocery planning feel like administration.
- Chatbot-first AI interfaces that require users to compose prompts for routine actions.
- Exact inventory systems that ask users to record every item consumed.
- Autonomous purchasing or opaque recommendations that obscure why an action was taken.
- Generic AI styling: decorative gradients, glass surfaces, excessive cards, and gratuitous animation.

## Design Principles

1. **Memory before management.** The product should remember patterns and reduce input, not create a second household bookkeeping job.
2. **Ask only when useful.** Consolidate uncertain products into a calm review instead of generating frequent individual alerts.
3. **One next action.** Every primary screen should make the next household task obvious: review, add, shop, or finish.
4. **Earn trust through clarity.** Say "usually bought every 7 days," not "you are out of milk." Make corrections quick and visible.
5. **Work where shopping happens.** The active shop must remain fast, legible, collaborative, and offline-capable in a supermarket aisle.
6. **Keep spending subordinate to the shop.** Budgets help a household decide; they do not turn the product into a finance dashboard.
7. **Focus the market, preserve the seam.** Ship UK-specific copy and receipt behaviour first, while currency, locale, time zone, and receipt parsing remain explicit market-aware inputs.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The interface must support Dynamic Type and Android font scaling, screen readers, reduced motion, increased contrast, dark appearance, and one-handed operation. Text must meet at least 4.5:1 contrast, large text and meaningful controls at least 3:1, and information must never rely on colour alone. Touch targets must be at least 44 by 44 points on iOS and 48 by 48 dp on Android. Core planning and shopping actions must remain understandable with long names, translated copy, missing prices, uncertain predictions, and intermittent connectivity.
